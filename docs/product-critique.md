# Product critique: a useful window into market decisions

## Recommendation

Serve distributor and procurement teams first. Their purchasing decisions map most directly onto reported restocking expenditure and remote ordering, and the calculator leads to a concrete follow-up question. This is a product hypothesis, not demonstrated buyer demand. Keep financial institutions and research/development partners as secondary discovery paths until interviews and paid pilots show a stronger use case.

The product's value should be a shorter route from a decision to credible field evidence. A visitor should leave with a question, the assumptions behind it, the evidence already available and a feasible next step. Additional sliders alone do not create that value.

## What this version does well

- Evidence and assumptions are visibly separated. The three explorers carry real current aggregate findings into a scoped enquiry without pretending the data predict behavior.
- Quote-first field briefs create a testable paid service before investing in automated reports or a payment funnel. The customer can agree scope, price and timing before work starts.
- The longitudinal watch makes the next research need visible while leaving unobserved periods empty. A genuine panel is a future asset worth funding; a speculative curve would weaken trust.
- Topic-specific, confirmed notifications offer a reason to return. QA revisions are distinguished from economic findings.

## Limits that matter

The current snapshot contains 115 initial observations after the owner approved including 27 identity-review observations with warnings and recovery of one shifted row. Possible repeat submissions remain unresolved; the expanded denominator is an inclusion-policy change, not economic change. Identities are provisional, the geography is configured at cohort level, and no reviewed matched panel is published. It is not a representative market sample. Separate aggregate signals do not establish which businesses share two characteristics. Reported expenditure changes do not establish supplier-price inflation. There is no verified demand, profit, sales, repayment or causal impact model.

Three audience explorers risk diluting the first buyer story. The financial-institution calculator is operational planning, not underwriting; the research calculator is visit budgeting, not a study-design service. Keep a distributor-led entry point and measure whether the other paths attract qualified questions before expanding them.

The paid service still needs a named owner for scoping, consent, field operations, editorial QA, delivery and customer follow-up. Email infrastructure does not create that service. A quote accepted, payment received and brief delivered are separate milestones; none is established by a form submission.

Daily digest processing is adequate only for a small early cohort. Live deployment still needs server credentials, a verified sending domain, durable storage, privacy/processor review, an owned-inbox delivery test, delivery monitoring and scheduler verification. Provider acceptance is not delivery. Browser push, payments, a customer portal, bounce handling and an admin dashboard are not implemented.

## Build next, in this order

1. Interview 5–10 distributor/procurement decision makers around their most recent restocking decision. Ask what uncertainty delayed action, what they already buy to resolve it, and what evidence would change the decision. Do not lead with the feature list.
2. Sell and fulfil one tightly scoped field brief. Define one decision, one market, permitted data, measures, deliverable and agreed timing. Review willingness to pay, cost to fulfil and whether the result changed a decision before automating pricing.
3. Resolve the 27 identity-review flags with source owners. Establish verified canonical identities and documented return-visit consent. Predefine consistent measures and visit windows; track refusals and attrition. Build the panel that makes repeat visits valuable.
4. Add a simple operator dashboard, delivery/bounce monitoring and an explicit quote/approval ledger once real requests justify them. Add payment only after the commercial terms and fulfilment process work.
5. Publish small, dated change notes explaining new evidence and limitations. Let customers submit the next question, with clear consent, before adding more scenario types or market coverage.

## Success measures to agree before launch

Track qualified enquiry → scope conversation → quote → customer approval → paid brief → delivered brief → decision usefulness → repeat purchase. Keep numbers and denominators explicit. Measure fulfilment hours and gross margin per brief alongside conversion. For notifications, measure confirmed topic opt-ins, useful replies, removals and delivery failures; avoid treating raw signup counts as value.

For evidence, track identity resolution, consented revisit coverage, complete matched pairs per measure, attrition, source freshness and QA corrections. A growing row count is not the same as a growing longitudinal memory.

No pricing, revenue forecast, demand estimate or new economic finding is asserted in this critique. Those are outcomes to validate through customer and field work.
